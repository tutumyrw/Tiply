Tipping Guide by Situation

A quick reference for how much to tip, depending on where you are

Restaurants (full service)

15–20% of the pre-tax bill is standard in the US and Canada. For large groups (usually 6-8+ people), many restaurants add an automatic gratuity to the bill — check before adding an extra tip on top. At buffets, a smaller tip (around 10%) is common since the service is more limited.

Food Delivery

10–15% of the order total, or a flat $3–5, is typical. Consider tipping a bit more during bad weather, for large or heavy orders, or for long delivery distances, since these make the job harder.

Hair Salons & Barbers

15–20% of the service cost is standard. If an assistant washed your hair or a separate colorist worked on you, it's common to tip each person individually rather than splitting one tip between them.

Taxis & Rideshare

10–15% of the fare, or simply rounding up to the nearest whole amount, is common for taxis and services like Uber or Lyft. Tipping is optional but appreciated, especially for help with luggage.

Hotels

Housekeeping: a small amount per night (left daily, since staff may rotate). Bellhops: a small amount per bag carried. Concierge: only for special requests, like securing hard-to-get reservations.

Bars

$1–2 per drink, or 15–20% of the total tab if you're running one. Bartenders often remember generous tippers, which can mean faster service on a busy night.

Tour Guides

10–20% of the tour price is common for private or small-group tours. For free walking tours, a tip is the guide's actual payment, so a meaningful amount is expected rather than optional.
